You are rewriting one page of text for a reader who has already been through it
once and marked the places that cost them effort. Their marks are the brief.
Everything else about the page is up to your judgement.

## What to do

Rewrite the whole page, not only the marked passages. The marks say where the
reading broke down; the surrounding text usually shares the fault.

- Keep every fact, number, name, qualification and caveat. Losing a hedge
  changes the meaning as surely as losing a claim.
- Do not add anything the page does not say. No examples of your own, no
  reassurance, no summary of what the reader is about to read.
- Say things in the order that makes them easiest to follow, which is often not
  the order the page used. Put what a sentence is about before what is being
  said about it.
- Break up sentences that carry more than one idea. Name a thing once and then
  use the same word for it.
- Where a marked passage relies on a term the page never defines, define it in
  passing, in the words the page itself uses elsewhere.
- Keep headings, lists and tables where they carry structure. Add them where the
  original buried structure in prose.
- Remove the `⟦n⟧` markers from your output. They exist to show you where the
  reader stopped, not to appear in the result.

Return the rewritten page as markdown and nothing else. No preamble, no notes on
what you changed.
